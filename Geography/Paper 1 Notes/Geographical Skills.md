# Geographical Skills

## **Extra content added**

**AQA assesses geographical skills across all three written papers. See the [AQA Geographical Skills specification](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/geography/gcse/geography-8035/specification/subject-content/geographical-skills).**

### **Cartographic skills**

**Use latitude and longitude; identify and describe patterns and distributions; interpret physical and human features and their relationships on maps.**

**For OS maps, practise four- and six-figure grid references, scale, straight and curved distances, direction, gradient, contours and spot heights. Identify relief, drainage, settlement, transport and land use; interpret cross-sections, transects, photographs and sketch maps; annotate diagrams and maps.**

### **Graphical skills**

**Choose, construct, complete and interpret line, bar, pie, divided-bar and scatter graphs; histograms; population pyramids; choropleth, isoline, dot, proportional-symbol, desire-line and flow-line maps. Use scales and gradients correctly and extract information from graphs, maps and charts.**

### **Numerical and statistical skills**

**Work with number, area, scale, units, ratios, proportions, magnitude and frequency. Calculate mean, median, mode, range, quartiles, interquartile range, percentage increase and decrease, and percentiles. Read and interpret cumulative frequency where relevant.**

**For scatter graphs, describe relationships, draw a line of best fit, make predictions, and interpolate or extrapolate cautiously. Identify limitations or misleading choices in statistical presentation. Draw conclusions supported by data.**

### **Data and geographical communication**

**Interpret qualitative and quantitative evidence from maps, fieldwork, GIS, satellite images, photographs, graphs, written sources and statistics. Distinguish primary from secondary data. Communicate findings clearly, analyse evidence, evaluate limitations and reach a supported conclusion.**


**AQA map skills include using and comparing OS maps at 1:25,000 and 1:50,000 scales; describing physical features shown on large-scale maps; and annotating or drawing field sketches from maps, photographs and field observations. Use evidence to support comparisons and conclusions.**
