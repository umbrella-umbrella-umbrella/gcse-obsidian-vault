# Physical Case Studies

## Typhoon Haiyan

- 6300 people dead, 30,000 fishing boats destroyed (livelihoods lost, damage to economy)
- Riots and looting broke out in Tacloban (destroys local businesses, damages economy)
- 90% Tacloban destroyed (livelihoods lost, damage to economy)
- Landslides and flooding cut off roads (increased death toll, slowed rescue efforts)
- International aid was sent (food, water, shelter)
- 1200 evacuation centers established (people can be protected from harm)
- Oxfam replaced the 30K fishing boats from earlier (also helped people’s lives go back to normal and build back the economy)
- Houses were rebuilt away from areas with high flood risk (This also protects the area in the future if a similar storm hit)

## Somerset Level Floods

- Kids couldn’t go to school (loss of education)
- Parents couldn’t go to work (reducing income and disrupting livelihoods)
- 16 farms had to be evacuated (This also affects habitats and food production, causing food prices nationally to increase)
- 14,000 hectares of farmland was underwater for 3–4 weeks, contributing to higher food prices and environmental impacts.
- Massive insurance payouts (businesses damaged)
- Locals will have a hard time selling their houses in the future
- Massive pipes were used to pump water out of the area (at a rate of 10 tonnes per second)
- Local people used boats and tractors to get around
- A 5-mile stretch of the rivers Tone and Parret were dredged, increasing water capacity by 40% (cost them 5 million pounds)
- 20 million pound flood action plan to improve the flood resistance of the area
- Rivers hadn’t been dredged in 20 years (they were asking for it – also it decreased the river capacity so less excess water was needed for the rivers to overflow)
- Extreme rainfall over the 2013-2014 winter (main cause since there was too much water for the rivers to handle – also the soil was very saturated, meaning that it couldn’t hold any more water)
- Somerset levels themselves are flat (few meters above sea level) so they flood very often anyway

## Gorkha earthquake (Nepal)

- 7.9 magnitude earthquake
- 7000 schools destroyed (disrupting education and future workforce development)
- $5 billion in damages
- Khumbu icefall on Mount Everest killed 19 climbers
- 500,000 tents were provided
- Field hospitals were established (lowered death toll, lowered risk of infection etc.)
- UK, India and China sent aid
- Those 7000 schools from earlier were rebuilt (allowing children to return to education and develop skills for future employment)
- Stricter building codes were established (reducing the risk of building collapse in future earthquakes)

## Tohoku Earthquake

- 9.0 magnitude earthquake off the coast of Japan
- 4.4 million people were left without electricity (disrupting communications)
- 1.5 million people were left without running water (increasing the risk of dehydration)
- An ice shelf broke in Antarctica due to the earthquake
- 330,000 people left homeless
- 38m high tsunami
- Government sent out warnings (helping to reduce deaths)
- Government requested international aid (including food, water and shelter)
- Military used mass graves to bury dead people (helping to prevent the spread of disease)
- Toyota and Sony permanently relocated some of their operations elsewhere (less jobs, economic damage)
- Those 330K homes were rebuilt for people who lost their houses (the reconstruction helped residents resume their lives and supported the local economy)
