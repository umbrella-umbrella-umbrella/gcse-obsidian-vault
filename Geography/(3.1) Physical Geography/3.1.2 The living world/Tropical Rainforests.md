# Tropical Rainforests
- ### Climate
	- Very wet with over 2,000 mm of rainfall per year.
	- Very warm with an average daily temperature of 28°C. 
		- The temperature never drops below 20°C and rarely exceeds 35°C.
	- The atmosphere is hot and humid.
	- The climate is consistent all year round. There are no seasons.
- ### Soil
	- Most of the soil is not very fertile.
	- A thin layer of fertile soil is found at the surface where the dead leaves decompose.
	- It is red in colour because it is rich in iron.
	- Due to heavy rainfall the nutrients are quickly washed out of the soil.
- ### Structure 
	1. Ground Layer
	2. Shrub Layer
	3. Under canopy
	4. (main) Canopy
	5. Emergents

## Adaptations - Animal + Plants
- ### Animals 
	- ### Draco Lizard
		- Has wings – can glide 100ft per leap 
		- Has camouflage to look like tree bark so it’s less likely that predators see it and therefore increase its changes of living
		- Has its main food source in trees (ants), avoiding the forest floor where all the predators are, increasing its chance of surviving
		
	- ### Spider Monkey
		- Has a prehensile tail, so it it loses footing it can catch itself on a tree trunk or vine, stopping it from falling to its death on the forest floor.
		- Travels in family groups, so if one gets stuck the others can help it, decreasing the chance of it falling, also makes it seem more threatening to predators 
		- Spider monkeys live and feed mainly in the forest canopy. Their prehensile tails help them move between branches and reach food.
	
- ### Plants
	- Drip tips - plants have leaves with pointy tips. This allows water to run off the leaves quickly without damaging or breaking them.
	- Emergents – Trees that grow taller than the rest to get better access to sunlight, so they grow faster.
	- Buttress roots – Large surface roots that support tall trees. Don't penetrate deep dur to soil being shallow and nutrient-poor.
## Deforestation

- ### Causes
	- Natural Causes
		- e.g Forest Fires, Tropical Storms, Lava Flow
	- Commercial farming 
		- Plantations of crops, palm oil or sugar cane destroy large areas of rainforest as they need to be cleared to provide room for the farm, demand for farmlan increases due to the demand for resources increasing because of the population.
	- Settlement 
		- Trees are cut down to supply materials to build houses and they are also cut down to make space for houses. Again, this will only increase as more houses are needed to house the growing population. 

- ### Effects Of Tropical Rainforest Deforestation
	- (-) Biodiversity loss 
		- if trees are cut down animals like birds lose their homes in the trees and have to relocate elsewhere, reducing biodiversity
	- (-) Infertile soil 
	- (-) On a global scale, deforestation causes climate change as cutting trees releases CO₂ and stops more CO₂ from being absorbed. This CO₂ traps heat since it is a greenhouse gas and warms the planet.
	- (+) Economic benefits
		- LICs can benefit from using the wood from trees in house building, and selling the wood which gives them income, so they can invest that into healthcare and education, benefiting the whole country.

## Choco Rainforest Deforestation – Causes, Effects

- ### Causes
	- The Choco rainforest is located mainly in Colombia and Ecuador – which combined produce 1.5 million tons of palm oil every year – so they need to grow that somewhere.
	- Side-Causes
		- Road building – A named example is the Trans-Amazonian highway, which runs through parts of the Chocó rainforest.
		- Oil extraction – some trees have to be cleared to access oil in the ground

- ### Impacts

	- Indigenous tribes such as the Embera have lost land due to palm oil plantations
	- Residents of the town of Curvaradó have been forcibly displaced to make way for a palm oil plantation 
	- The Atrato river has been polluted due to toxic pesticides used in palm oil plantations
	- Animals like the Jaguar and spectacled bear have had their habitats destroyed due to palm oil plantations

## Value of Rainforests
- ### Global 
	- 25% of all modern medicines derive from tropical rainforests. 
		- Without them, potential cures for diseases will be destroyed, hurting modern populations.
	- Tropical rainforests support production of crops such as bananas and coffee.
	- Latosol soils have low fertility and support limited plant growth.
	- Deforestation reduces CO₂ absorption, increasing greenhouse gas concentrations and climate change.
- ### Local
	- Direct source of livelihood to local communities, providing essential food, fresh water and building materials.
	- Deep cultural significance to indigenous groups

- ## Strategies To Reduce Deforestation

	- We don't have the right to tell other countries what to do with their resources, but we can advise them.
	
	- ## Selective Logging And Replanting
		- Loggers only remove the most valuable trees in the forest without damaging surrounding trees
		- Trees are then replanted as secondary forest
		- (+) More trees remain
		- (-) Secondary Forest won't be as biodiverse as the forest was initially
	
	- ### Agreements + Debt reduction
		- Agreements to protect tropical rainforests have been made between different countries through debt-for-nature swaps. 
		- This is when a country which is owed money by another country cancels part of the debt if an agreement is made by the debtor country to ensure the conservation of its tropical rainforests.
	
	- ### Education
		- Where locals are taught the importance of rainforests and how to protect them
		- (+) Teaches the locals
		- (-) Small-scale, some people may not listen
	
	- ### Ecotourism
		- Resorts which employ local people and earn money
		- (+) Employs locals
		- (-) Expensive
