### Method:
1. Using a ruler, draw a horizontal pencil line 2cm away from the short edge of the chromatography paper. Mark 5 pencil spots at equal distance intervals across the line, keeping at least 2cm away from each one.
2. Use a glass capillary tube to put a small spot of each known food colouring and the unknown one on the 5 pencil spots. The dyes should be no more than 5mm in diameter. Label them in pencil.
3. Pour water into the beaker to a depth of no more than 1 cm.
4. Attach the edge of the paper furthest from the spots to the glass rod so that when the rod is rested on the top edge the bottom edge of the paper dips into the water. ==Ensure that the pencil line is above the water surface and the sides do not touch the beaker wall.==
5. Without disturbing the beaker wait for the water solvent to travel at least 3/4 of the way up the paper. When it does carefully remove it and draw another pencil line on the dry part of the paper as close to the wet edge as possible.
6. Hang the paper to dry thoroughly.    
7. Measure the distance in mm between the two pencil lines. This is the distance travelled by the water solvent. Write the same distance in the table below for each colouring.
8. For each of the four known colours measure the distance in mm from the bottom line to the centre of each spot. Write each measurement in the table.
