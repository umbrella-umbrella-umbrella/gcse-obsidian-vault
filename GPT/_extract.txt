Security issues threaten safety and privacy of data, especially the use of open 
networks. 
Security issues with open networks
Open networks are vulnerable because data is 
unencrypted:
∙ Eavesdropping: Others can intercept data 
sent over the network.
∙ Man-in-the-middle attacks: Hackers can 
intercept or modify communications.
∙ Malware distribution: Devices can be 
infected by malicious users on the same 
network.
∙ Unauthorised access: Any device can connect 
without authentication, potentially accessing 
shared ﬁles.
Learning in Context
Mitigation strategies:
• Use a VPN when 
connected to open 
Wi-Fi.
• Avoid sensitive 
transactions (banking, 
passwords) on open 
networks.
• Enable ﬁrewalls and 
security software.
Using public Wi-Fi at a café, airport, or hotel
• Public Wi-Fi is convenient but often not secure,

Network performance
Performance issues affect the speed, reliability, and usability of the network and 
in particular, ad hoc networks. 
Performance issues with ad hoc networks
Ad hoc networks often have limited performance 
compared to traditional networks:
• Limited range: Wi-Fi hotspots only cover a 
small area.
• Shared bandwidth: Multiple devices using the 
same connection can slow speeds.
• Interference: Other wireless devices 
(microwaves, Bluetooth, nearby networks) can 
reduce performance.
• Device dependency: If the host device (e.g., 
smartphone) moves or loses power, the 
network is disrupted.
Learning in Context

Data protection
Data protection refers to the legal and ethical frameworks designed to 
safeguard personal information from unauthorised access, misuse, or 
breaches. 
Data protection principles
Activity – Respecting data and technology
Complete Activity A
What is a data subject?
A data subject is any individual 
whose personal information is 
collected, processed, or stored by 
an organisation, including media 
producers. Individuals have rights 
over their personal data
Data protection principles
• Lawful processing  
• Collected only for speciﬁc 
purpose  
• Only needed information is 
collected  
• Should be accurate  
• Kept only as long as is 
necessary  
• Data subject rights  
• Protected  
• Data not transferred to 
countries with less 
protection. 
Data and the use of the internet
• The right to be forgotten (i.e. 
Individuals may request their 
data to be deleted) 
• Appropriate and legal use of 
cookies and other transactional 
data. 
Lawful processing
Data must be collected and used only when there is a valid legal 
reason.
Collected only for a speciﬁc purpose
Data must be collected for a clear reason and only used for that 
reason.
Only needed information is collected (data minimisation)
Organisations should collect the minimum data necessary.
Should be accurate
Personal data must be kept up to date and corrected when wrong.
Kept only as long as necessary (storage limitation)
Data should not be kept longer than needed.
Protected (security and conﬁdentiality)
Data must be kept safe from loss, hacking, or misuse.
Data not transferred to countries with less protection
Personal data should only be transferred to countries with strong data 
protection laws or with proper safeguards in place.

Criminal use of computer systems
Criminal use of computer systems” refers to situations where 
computers, networks, or digital devices are used to carry out 
activities that break the law. This can involve using a computer as the 
tool to commit a crime, the target of the crime, or both.
Examples of Computer misuse
Activity – Respecting data and technology
Complete Activity B
What does this mean?
It covers actions such as:
• accessing computer systems or 
accounts without permission
• viewing, copying, or stealing data 
unlawfully
• changing, deleting, or damaging 
ﬁles or software
• creating or spreading malware 
(viruses, ransomware, spyware, 
etc.)
• disrupting systems or networks
• using computers to support fraud, 
scams, or identity theft
Unauthorised access (Hacking)
This is when someone accesses a computer system, 
network, account, or data without permission, even if they 
don’t change or delete anything
Unauthorised modiﬁcation of materials
This is when someone changes, deletes, or damages data or 
software without permission. This includes actions that cause 
disruption.
Creation of malware
This is when someone designs or writes malicious software 
intended to damage systems, steal data. spy on users, 
disrupt services and gain unauthorised access
Intentional spreading of malware
This is when someone deliberately distributes malware to 
infect computers or networks.
The intent to cause harm or disruption makes this illegal.
These activities are illegal 
because they…
• Break privacy laws
• Damage systems or data
• Put people or 
organisations at risk
They are often covered by 
laws such as the Computer 
Misuse Act (UK) or similar 
legislation in other 
countries.
