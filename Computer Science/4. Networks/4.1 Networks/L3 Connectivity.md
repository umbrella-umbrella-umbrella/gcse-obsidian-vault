### Connectivity

Wired vs wireless
All methods of transmitting data can be considered as either wired or
wireless.
The difference is simple:
- wired transmission involves making a physical connection from one
device to another, for example using a USB or ethernet cable
- wireless transmission uses no physical connection. Instead it uses
radio waves, for example Bluetooth®, Airdrop, and NFC.

### Connectivity

Cables
Wired transmission commonly
uses copper or fibre-optic cables.
Copper is more common as it has
been used to transmit data since
the telegraph was invented over
150 years ago.
They can also be designed as
Unshielded Twisted Pair (UTP)

connected computers together.
Fibre-optic cable is a modern
alternative that has vastly higher

### Connectivity

Broadband
Broadband is a type of high-capacity internet connectivity that is
being rolled out across the country. Many of you will have broadband
internet at home.
Typically for homes, broadband is implemented as fibre to the
cabinet (FTTC). In this approach, fibre optic cables run to green
street cabinets (which you will probably have noticed). Copper cables
then link homes to the cabinets (usually over distances of less than a
mile).
The alternative, which is generally used for businesses, is fibre to
the premises (FTTP). In this approach, the final stretch from the
cabinet to the business uses another fibre optic cable.

### Connectivity

Wrap up: you have learned how to…
✔ Differentiate between wired and wireless connectivity.
- The difference is in the type of connection between devices.
✔ Explain how data is transmitted along copper and fibre-optic cables.
- In copper cables, data is transmitted as electrical pulses.
- In fibre optic cables, data is transmitted as pulses of light.

examples of their use.
