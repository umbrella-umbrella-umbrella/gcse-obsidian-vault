# Respecting data and technology

### Data protection
- **Data protection** refers to legal and ethical frameworks that safeguard personal information from unauthorised access, misuse, or breaches.
- A **data subject** is an individual whose personal information is collected, processed, or stored by an organisation. Individuals have rights over their personal data.

### Data protection principles
- Process data lawfully, with a valid legal reason.
- Collect it for a clear, specific purpose and use it only for that purpose.
- Collect only the minimum information needed (data minimisation).
- Keep personal data accurate and correct it when it is wrong.
- Keep data only as long as necessary (storage limitation).
- Protect it from loss, hacking, or misuse (security and confidentiality).
- Transfer it only to countries with suitable data protection laws or safeguards.
- Respect data subject rights, including the right to request deletion in relevant circumstances.
- Use cookies and other transactional data appropriately and legally.

### Criminal use of computer systems
- Computers, networks, or digital devices can be used as tools for crime, be the target of crime, or both.
- Examples include unauthorised access to systems or accounts, unlawfully viewing or stealing data, changing or damaging files or software, creating or spreading malware, disrupting systems, and supporting fraud or identity theft.
- **Unauthorised access (hacking):** Accessing a system, network, account, or data without permission, even if nothing is changed.
- **Unauthorised modification:** Changing, deleting, or damaging data or software without permission.
- Creating or intentionally spreading malware can damage systems, steal data, spy on users, disrupt services, or enable unauthorised access.
- Such activities can break privacy laws, damage systems and data, and put people and organisations at risk. The source identifies the UK Computer Misuse Act as one law that covers these activities.
