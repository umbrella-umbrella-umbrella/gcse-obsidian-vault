# Equal access to IT
- **Equal access to IT** means everyone has a fair and reasonable opportunity to use digital technology, online services, and information systems.
- When people are excluded, a **digital divide** is created: the gap between people with access to digital technologies, fast internet, and digital skills and those without them.

### Net neutrality
- **Net neutrality** is the principle that internet service providers (ISPs) should treat all internet traffic equally.
- Websites, apps, and online services should have the same priority and access speed; users, rather than providers, decide what content to access.

### Benefits of equal access
- **Individuals:** Access to online education, remote work, employment opportunities, healthcare, and banking.
- **Organisations:** A larger talent pool, better productivity and customer communication, and more innovation and creativity.
- **Society:** Reduced inequality, economic growth, improved education, and more connected communities.

### Legislation and standards
- The **Equality Act** protects against discrimination and supports fair access to services, employment, and digital systems, including reasonable adjustments.
- The **Digital Economy Act** supports digital inclusion through access to affordable broadband and modern communications services.
- The **World Wide Web Consortium (W3C)** publishes internationally recognised accessibility standards.
