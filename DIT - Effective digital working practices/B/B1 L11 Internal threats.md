# Internal threats
- An **internal threat** (or insider threat) is a security risk that comes from within an organisation rather than from external attackers.
- People who may create an internal threat include employees, students, contractors, volunteers, and temporary staff who already have authorised access.

### Examples

| Internal threat | How it can cause harm |
| --- | --- |
| Unintentional disclosure | A user accidentally shares sensitive information because of human error or lack of awareness. |
| Intentional theft or leaking | An employee takes, copies, or shares confidential data for personal gain or malicious reasons. |
| Overriding security controls | Users disable or bypass features they find inconvenient. |
| Portable storage devices | USB sticks may introduce malware or be lost, exposing data. |
| Downloads from the internet | Files from unverified sources may introduce malware or spyware. |
| Untrustworthy websites | May install malware or trick users into revealing personal information. |

### Why internal threats are dangerous
- Insiders already have access permissions.
- Their actions may not trigger security alerts.
- They understand how the organisation's systems work.
- Damage can happen quickly and quietly.
